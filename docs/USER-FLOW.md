# How Bidline is used

Claude reads; Bidline keeps the record, does the maths and gets the award approved.

## Who is involved

| Who | What they do in Bidline |
|---|---|
| Buyer (daily user) | Runs quote rounds: sets up the event, collects quotes, checks prices, chases suppliers, drafts the award. |
| Head of Procurement (pays) | Sees every event, savings against budget, spend and the audit log. Invites the team and sets approval rules. |
| Approver, often the CFO | Gets one link, sees the award with every price sourced, and approves or rejects. |
| Claude (the buyer's own) | Reads supplier files and does work when asked, through Bidline's tools, as the buyer and with the buyer's role. |
| Suppliers | Change nothing. They keep replying by email, WhatsApp or file. |

## First time (about 3 minutes)

1. **Sign in.** Bidline asks one question: use demo data or your own files?
2. **Demo data** loads three fictional events. **Own files** offers three ways to start: tell Claude, fill a short form, or use a template.
3. **Connect Claude.** Copy your private link (popup or Ask Claude), add it in Claude as a custom connector, and switch Bidline on in a chat.
4. **Home** opens: what needs attention, quotes due, savings, and your own to-dos.

## One sourcing event, start to finish

| Step | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Set up | Buyer or Claude | "Create a sourcing event for 40 chairs, due in two weeks, invite Featherline." The checklist shows what is missing before it goes out. |
| 2. Quotes arrive | Suppliers | Replies come in any format. The buyer drops the files into Documents. |
| 3. Read | Claude | "Read every unread supplier document and submit the quotes." Each price arrives with the supplier's exact words. |
| 4. Compare | Bidline | Units, packs, currency and warranty are converted in code. Unusual prices are flagged; doubtful ones stay out of totals. |
| 5. Check | Buyer | Clicks a price to see its source, corrects it with a reason, comments or @mentions a colleague. |
| 6. Chase | Buyer or Claude | Follow-ups list what each supplier still owes and send from the buyer's own mailbox. Reminders go out automatically. |
| 7. Award | Buyer, then approver | Bidline prices the options; the buyer drafts the award and sends one approval link. Steps by value apply. |
| 8. Record | Bidline | Every change is in a sealed audit log, tagged Claude or Person. Export to Excel any time. |

## A day with Bidline

| Time | What the buyer does | Before Bidline | With Bidline |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9:00 | Opens Home | Searches email for what came in overnight | Sees 2 new quotes, 1 overdue supplier, 1 award waiting |
| 9:15 | Two suppliers replied | Retypes two quotes into a spreadsheet: about an hour | Drops the files in; asks Claude to read them: a few minutes |
| 9:30 | Compares | Misses that one quote is per piece, not per box | The unit is converted and flagged; she checks the supplier's own words |
| 11:00 | Chases | Writes three follow-up emails by hand | Asks Claude to chase what is missing, due Friday |
| 14:00 | New request | Starts a fresh spreadsheet | Asks Claude to create the event from last quarter's template |
| 16:00 | Awards | Emails the CFO a spreadsheet; waits days | Sends one approval link; the CFO approves from a phone |
| 17:00 | Head of Procurement checks in | Asks for a status update | Opens spend analytics and the audit log |

## Why it helps (time and savings are targets to measure in pilots)

- **Buyer:** about a day saved per quote round, no retyping, no unit or currency traps.
- **Head of Procurement:** the right supplier wins, savings against budget are visible, the team works one way.
- **CFO:** approves in one sitting, with every number traced to the supplier's words and nobody approving their own award.
- **Auditor:** a sealed record of who changed what and when, months later.
